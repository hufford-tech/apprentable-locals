| **Frequently Asked Questions**<br>When will applications for the apprenticeship programs open up again?<br>Applications are closed. We will post an opening announcement on our website 1 month in advance of when they open back up.<br>What is an electrical apprenticeship?<br>An electrical apprenticeship is a structured training program where individuals learn the concepts and skills necessary to become a licensed electrician.  An apprenticeship consists of two parts: paid on-the-job training (OJT) and related training (in the classroom). <br>Is it hard to get into the program?<br>Yes, this is a very competitive program to get into.  You must meet minimum age and educational requirements.  Hiring is dependent on current employment needs.  Things that can help your chances are a strong education and work history.  A resume, letters of recommendation and copies of applicable certifications can help as well. <br>How do I apply?<br>Please fill out the [Application Request Form](https://unionly.io/o/cjatc/request-for-application) and pay the $35 application fee for each program you would like to apply for **.** We will mail you the application.  Fill out the application and return with your transcripts.  You can return them by mail or in person. <br>Can I apply in Prineville?<br>No, the Prineville Training Center does not accept applications.  We administer the Aptitude Test and conduct interviews in Prineville for applicants that live in Central Oregon. We also hold classes in Prineville for apprentices and JW CEUs. <br>Can I request the application by phone?<br>No, you must fill out the [Application Request Form](https://unionly.io/o/cjatc/request-for-application). <br>How much does it cost to apply?<br>There is a $35 application fee.<br>What should I bring if I apply in person?<br>You should bring all qualifying documents such as high school transcripts, college transcripts, and GED with scores.  If you are a veteran, you need to include your DD214. Additionally, you may choose to provide a resume, letters of recommendation and applicable certifications. You need to have all documents together when you submit your application to be qualified.  You will be submitting the documents, we do not make copies for applicants, nor return them to the applicants.<br>What should I mail in with my application?<br>You should mail all qualifying documents such as high school transcripts, college transcripts, and GED with scores.  If you are a veteran, you need to include your DD214. Additionally, you may choose to provide a resume, letters of recommendation and applicable certifications. You need to have all documents together when you submit your application to be qualified.  You will be submitting the documents, we do not make copies for applicants, nor return them to the applicants.<br>How do I get my transcripts?<br>You will need to contact your school or school district.  Many transcript requests can only be made online.  You will need to allow plenty of time to get the transcripts. The schools may mail the transcripts directly to us, or you may provide them.  If you are having your transcripts sent directly from the school, please include a note indicating as such with your application.  Official transcripts are preferred and may be required if necessary due to poor copies or missing information. <br>Screen shots and pictures of documents are not accepted. We cannot call your school to request them.  We cannot print or make copies of your documents. It is your responsibility to provide your transcripts to this office.  <br>Do you need official transcripts?<br>Official transcripts are preferred and may be required if necessary due to poor copies or missing information.  Unofficial will be accepted as long as they are a good, complete copy. <br>I received my transcripts and see that I am not qualified. How can I become qualified now?<br>If you do not have at least 1 year of Algebra or higher on high school transcripts, or 1 term of Algebra or higher on college transcripts, you can either:<br>- Take a college algebra class (Math 60 minimum)<br>- Take the [Tech Math](https://www.cjatc.org/index.cfm?zone=/unionactive/private_view_page.cfm&page=Tech20Math20Course) course<br>- Take a college placement test and place into Intermediate Algebra (Math 95) [Mt.Hood CC Tests](https://www.mhcc.edu/CollegePlacementTestRequestForm/)<br>If you do not have at least a 2.0 cumulative GPA, or your GED score is below 255/2550/600 (depending on when you tested), you can take the [GED Ready test](http://www.gedmarketplace.com/GED-Ready-The-Official-Practice-Test-p/2014_optm.htm).  You will need to take all 4 tests with a score of 150 or better.  Print and submit the My Scores page with your application and transcripts.  <br>If you have an Associate's Degree or higher, that will meet the graduation/GED/gpa requirements as well. You will need to submit the transcripts that show graduation and will still need to show the algebra requirement. <br>I am a senior in high school. Can I apply before I graduate?<br>You need your complete transcripts showing graduation before you can apply.<br>When will I know if I've been accepted?<br>We will send you an email when we process the applications and have the aptitude test scheduled for you.  If you are qualified, you will be invited to take the aptitude test.  If you pass the aptitude test, you will be scheduled for an interview.  After the interview, applicants are ranked on the eligible-to-hire list according to the score received through the hiring process.  We hire the top applicant when we have an opening.  That means we have a job for the top person and will assign them to a contractor.  School will be scheduled for you sometime after being assigned to a contractor. <br>If you are not qualified, a letter will be mailed to you that will explain why.  You are welcome to reapply. <br>I submitted my application but haven't heard anything. What's next?<br>We send notices to take the aptitude test to all qualified applicants when we process applications.  It can be a few months after submitting your application.  After the aptitude test, we send notices to interview or let you know that you did not pass.  Approximately 2 weeks after the interviews, we send rank letters.  <br>What's on the Aptitude Test?<br>Reading comprehension and math.  You should plan on being here for about 3 ½ hours for the test. <br>Is there any study material for the test?<br>We do not provide study materials.  There are a couple of courses available for a cost. Please visit the [electrical training ALLIANCE](http://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/SamplePage) for more test information. <br>[Tech Math](https://www.cjatc.org/index.cfm?zone=/unionactive/private_view_page.cfm&page=Tech20Math20Course)<br>[Electric Prep](https://www.electricprep.com/)<br>Other recommended courses would be through your local community college or self-study.<br>When do classes start?<br>When you are selected to begin the apprenticeship, you will start work (OJT) right away and then be scheduled to start classes according to our school schedule.<br>What is school like?<br>You can expect to attend classes during the day, once per week, for a 10-week term.  Homework is done online.  You must have access to a computer and the internet.  You must pass classes to continue in the program.  Our classes are held at our Training Centers, either in Tangent or Prineville, taught by licensed journey-level electricians.<br>How much does school cost?<br>Tuition is $300 per year.<br>How much money will I make?<br>Apprentices start at a specified percentage of journeyman wages and work their way up during their apprenticeship.  There is also a benefit package that is in addition to wages.  The benefit package includes family health insurance and pensions. [wagesandbenefits.pdf](https://www.cjatc.org/docs/wagesandbenefits.pdf)<br>Is work full time?<br>Apprentices usually work pretty steadily, but in all aspects of construction, there can be times of unemployment.  There are downtimes between jobs or due to a slow economy.  We suggest that apprentices live within their means and plan for downtimes.  Be fiscally responsible! Save money!<br>Overtime is also a possibility when things are very busy.  <br>A typical work week is 40 hours M-F 7 am-3:30 pm. <br>Apprentices will miss one day per week of work during the scheduled school term.<br>Will I join the union?<br>Yes.  When you are hired as an apprentice for your first job, you will join the union.  <br>Can I find my own work?<br>No, we are responsible for your On the Job Training (OJT) assignments. |
