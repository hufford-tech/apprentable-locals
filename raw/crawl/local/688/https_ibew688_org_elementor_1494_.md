Work has slowed a bit with layoffs at 4 local contractors. RMF Nooter has

added a few at the AK Steel Plant. For 2022, solar will headline the work,

provided our contractors are able to secure the work. There are 3 large solar field

in the permitting phase of development. Crawford Co (Sycamore Creek) had a

public hearing in July and I spoke in favor at that hearing. Marion Co (Marion

Solar LLC) had a public hearing Sept. 28, Dave Carnes, retiree Mike Huffman,

and I all spoke in favor of the Marion project. Wyandot Co (Tymochtee) will have

its hearing on Oct. 26, and it would be great if we could get someone who lives in

Wyandot county to speak on behalf of this project, Dave Carnes and I will be

there in support of this project as well, the hearings are short. We have also met

with a developer who is proposing a field in Morrow County and one in Knox

county. All this work is scheduled to begin in 2022 and and will continue through

at least 2024. These projects are necessary to bring in more big business, as a

majority of Fortune 500 companies have stated they want to be carbon neutral by

2030.

In Solidarity,

Carl Neutzling

688 The Wire: IBEW Local 688 Newsletter www.ibew688.org P.2

We are continuing our Organizing effort here at 688, and I am putting together a

Volunteer Organizing Committee, and I am hopeful that we will be able to really step up

our game with not only unrepresented electricians in our area, but also with the non-
signatory contractors. Some of the duties will involve hanging door hangers on

unrepresented doors, educating the unrepresented, getting out into the communities and

letting the community leaders know that we are more than just a “union”. We will be

holding bi-monthly meetings before our regular general meetings, each meeting should

be short, yet productive, involving brainstorming and game planning. I hope to have at

least 3-4 volunteers to get involved, but would love to have all of membership there.

I have made several Top-Down organizing efforts, trying to bring in more

contractors so that we can continue to do our first objective in our Constitution, “To

organize all workers in the entire electrical industry in the United States and Canada,

including all those in public utilities and electrical manufacturing, into local Unions” The

more organized workforce means greater benefits for our membership, and the industry

as a whole. With membership involvement, I feel that we can deliver on this objective. I

have reached out to and spoke with James Streng and Sam Long, owners of Strong

Electric of Ashland, John Carter of Carter Electric, and Bill Nichols of Nichols Electric of

Lexington.

Local 688 President Brian McPeek and myself attended COMET Training at Local

683, we will be holding a COMET class in January, 2022. If you have never attended

COMET training, the International Office request that all members be trained in COMET.

I also attended Employapalooza Job Fair at the Mid-Ohio Conference Center, spoke

with several people, unfortunately, none are currently in the industry. I encouraged them

to apply for the Apprenticeship and I followed up with each of them the following week.

I would like to thank those who showed up in support of solidarity for the Labor

Day Parade, Dave Carnes and family, Carl Neutzling and family, Will Perry, and Jeff

Phillips and family. All the Local Trade Unions were there in support as well. It was a

beautiful day to honor and recognize the Labor Movement and remember all those that

fought for the many things that we now take for granted. There was free carousel rides

after, along with free food, cotton candy, and even balloon animals.
