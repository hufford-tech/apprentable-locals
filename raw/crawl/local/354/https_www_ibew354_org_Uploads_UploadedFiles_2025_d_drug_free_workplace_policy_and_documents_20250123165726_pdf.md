## Purpose

The members of Local 354, IBEW, The Intermountain Chapter, <u>NECA</u>, the UETA, and other signatory contractors are 
committed to a workplace environment, which ensures the safety and encourages the personal health and productivity 
of all employees in our industry. We recognize that substance abuse in the workplace is a threat to the safety, health, 
and job performance of all employees. The goal of this policy is to balance respect for individuals with the need to 
maintain a safe, productive, alcohol and drug-free environment.

The IBEW and NECA jointly combine to form the Drug and Alcohol-Free Workplace Committee, hereafter known as 
the DFW Committee. They have established this policy to detect and remove abusers of alcohol and drugs from the 
workplace. The parties recognize the Employer’s right to adopt and implement a drug and alcohol policy subject to all 
applicable laws and regulations, procedural safeguards, scientific principles, and legitimate interests of privacy and 
confidentiality. However, the <u>Union</u> reserves the right to negotiate regarding the terms of the Employer’s policy before 
the policy is implemented by the Employer. When drug and alcohol testing is performed, all testing shall be conducted 
in accordance with the procedures outlined in the aforementioned policy.

This policy will be governed under the Administrative Procedures negotiated by the IBEW, NECA, and other signatory 
contractors. The DFW Committee reserves the right to change this policy and/or the Administrative Procedures. Any 
amendments to the policy or the Administrative Procedures shall become effective upon written notice to all parties. 
<u>The purpose of this document is to convey to employees the DFW Policy on drug and alcohol use in the workplace.</u>

## Scope

This substance abuse policy shall be the policy for all contributing employers and their bargaining unit employees.

As a condition of employment, employees are required to abide by this policy. Certain employees may also be subject 
to additional requirements that are mandated for their job duties under State and/or Federal regulations, including, but 
not limited to, DOD or DOT (FMCSA, FAA, FRA, FTA, PHMSA, USCG).

## Definitions

**A. “Alcohol”** means the intoxicating agent in beverage alcohol, ethyl alcohol, or other low molecular weight alcohols, 
including methyl and isopropyl alcohol.

**B. “Alcohol Testing”** means breath testing by a certified breath-alcohol technician, using a DOT approved breathtesting device.

**C. “Drugs” or “Controlled Substances”** means any drug including those assigned by 21 U.S.C. 802 and includes all 
substances listed on Schedule I, through Schedule V., as they may be revised from time-to-time (21 CFR 1308). They 
may include, but are not limited to:

1. Marijuana - THC

2. Cocaine

6. Barbiturates

3. Opioids.

7. Benzodiazepines

4. Phencyclidine (PCP)

8. Methadone

5. Amphetamines (Methamphetamine, Ecstasy, MDMA)

9. Propoxyphene (Urine samples)

10. Synthetic Opiates

**D. “Drug Testing” or “Drug Test ”** means scientific analysis for the presence of drugs or their metabolites in the human 
body.

**E. “Designated Employer Representative”** (DER) means person (s) authorized to receive confidential drug and 
alcohol information.

**F. “Employee”** means bargaining unit employees employed under all Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBA) and 
addendum negotiated between IBEW, and <u>NECA</u>.

**G. “Employer”** means any contributing contractor who is signatory to a collective bargaining agreement with IBEW 
Local 354.

**H. “Employee Assistance Program”** (EAP) means a designated provider of services for the purpose of drug and 
alcohol use assessment, provision of treatment options and plans coordinating referrals for employees who violate 
this policy. In addition, they provide ongoing follow-up and coordination of ongoing care. Currently, the EAP is 
Blomquist Hale Solutions.

**I. “Employer Property”** Means all facilities, job sites, vehicles and equipment that are leased, operated, or utilized by 
the employer or its employees for work-related purposes. This will include parking areas and driveways, lockers, 
toolboxes, or other related storage areas used by employees. It will also include other public or private property, 
facilities, vehicles, and equipment located away from the employer facility if the employee is present on such 
property for work-related purposes.

**J. “Employer Time”** means all compensable time.

**K. “Medical Review Officer”** (**MRO**) means a licensed physician with knowledge of controlled substance abuse 
disorders that is employed or used by the Third-Party Administrator * to conduct a review of drug test results.

---

**L. “Positive/Possible Test Result”** means any test result which meets any of the following criteria:

1. **Positive Alcohol Test** means test levels on both the initial test, and the confirmation test produce a result of 
.02 grams per 210 liters of breath for fitness for duty determination. A result of .04 grams per 210 liters of 
breath, or greater, will be determined as a violation of this policy.

2. **Adulterated/Invalid Test** means a specimen that contains a substance that is not expected to be present in 
human urine, or contains a substance expected to be present, but is at a concentration so high that it is not 
consistent with human urine.

3. **Substituted Test** means a specimen with creatinine and specific gravity values that are so diminished, or so 
divergent, that are not consistent with human urine.

4. **Refusal to Test** means that an employee/applicant failed to provide an adequate specimen due to any of the 
reasons listed below:

i) Failure to appear for any test within a reasonable time, as determined by the employer, <u>TPA</u>*  o r  E A P *,  
after being directed to take a test.

ii) Failure to provide a specimen or a sufficient amount of specimen when directed, unless the <u>MRO</u>* 
has verified a pre-existing medical condition, which explains why an adequate specimen cannot be 
provided.

iii) Failure to cooperate with any part of the collection or testing process.

**M. “Prospective Employee”** means any person who has made an application to become a Bargain Unit employee of an 
employer.

**N. “Random Testing”** means the unannounced drug and or alcohol testing of an employee who was selected by using 
a computer scientific seed generated selection method uninfluenced by any personal characteristic other than job 
category.

**O. “Reasonable Suspicion” or “For-Cause Testing”** means testing founded on any two contemporaneous visual 
behaviors or an articulated belief, based on recorded specific facts and reasonable inference drawn from those facts, 
that an employee is impaired, under the influence of, or has used drugs or alcohol at work. (See <u>Observed</u>
Behavior/Reasonable Suspicion Record*)

**P. “Sample” or “Specimen”** means any sample of urine, oral fluid, hair, blood, or breath used for controlled substance 
or alcohol testing.

**Q. “Third Party Administrator”** (TPA) A third administrator contracted to perform services on behalf of the DFW 
Committee’s Drug Free Workplace Program. Services currently provided by CODA.

**R. “Union”** shall mean all members of the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW).

**S. “Use”** means to illegally consume, sell, purchase, manufacture, distribute, report to work under the influence of, or 
be in the possession of drugs or alcohol at work. The term **“**use**”**shall also include the presence of drugs or alcohol 
in the body of an employee, including the presence as a metabolite, as indicated by a positive test, and the use of a 
prescription drug without a valid prescription from an authorizing physician or other healthcare provider.

## Prohibited Activities

It is a violation of this policy for any employee to use drugs or alcohol while on Employer time, conducting Employer 
business or on employer property. An employee shall be considered in violation of the policy if they have reported to 
work under the influence of drugs or alcohol.

<u>Events attended voluntarily are not considered to be covered by this policy.</u>

An employee shall be in violation of the policy if they report to work under the influence of, or have present in their body,
drugs, or alcohol. A drug test will be considered positive when the test result is verified by an <u>MRO</u>* as “positive” for 
drugs or their metabolites. An alcohol test will be considered positive when the test result is at or above 0.02. See the 
Definitions section, “Positive Test,” * for more specific criteria of what constitutes a positive test.

<u>Employer sponsored events attended voluntarily are not considered to be covered by this policy.</u>

The use of a prescription drug under the care of a physician that may impair the employee’s ability to safely perform 
their duties must be reported to the employee’s supervisor. It will be a violation of the policy to use a prescription drug 
without a valid prescription from an authorizing physician.

It is a violation of this policy to be in possession of or under the influence of drugs or alcohol while attending required 
training.

If an employee’s alcohol test is between 0.02-0.039, They will not be allowed to work for 24 hours, or one shift, and 
will be required to pass a return to duty breath alcohol test with a result below 0.02 grams per 210 liters of breath.

Any employee whose alcohol test is 0.02 or greater, but less than 0.04, for the second time in 12 consecutive 
months, shall be referred to the <u>EAP</u> for evaluation and will not be allowed to work for 24 hours, or one shift, the 
employer will be required to document the incident. This is not a violation unless the employee does not complete 
the EAP evaluation.

Any employee with an alcohol test of 0.04 or higher will be in violation of this policy and shall be referred to the EAP for 
evaluation.

Any employee, with a previous alcohol result of 0.04 or greater, whose alcohol test is 0.02 or greater for the second 
time in 12 consecutive months shall be in violation of this policy and shall be referred to the EAP for evaluation.

It is a violation of this policy for any employee, who is required to submit to a post-accident drug and/or alcohol test, 
to use any controlled substance or alcohol until they have received a post-accident drug and/or alcohol test, unless 
the controlled substance is by valid prescription or medical treatment.

It is a violation of this policy for an employee to refuse to submit a specimen when required to do so under the terms 
of this policy. Refusal to submit a specimen will be treated as a positive test result.

If the employee is unable to produce a urine specimen within the 3 hours due to medical circumstances, then the 
employee will report to the <u>MRO</u> (within 72 hours). The MRO will determine the status of the refusal to test in this 
circumstance.

---

Any employee who tampers with, substitutes, or adulterates a specimen for drug or al-cohol testing will be in violation 
of this policy. Any specimen reported by the <u>MRO</u> as substituted or adulterated will be treated as a positive test result.

It is a violation of this policy for any employee to fail to cooperate in the collection of a specimen for a drug and/or 
alcohol test which has been properly ordered. Failure to co-operate will be treated as a positive test result.

Once referred to the <u>EAP</u> for violation of this policy, the employee must comply with all counseling, rehabilitation, and 
aftercare as required by the EAP. If the employee fails to cooperate, the employee will be considered in violation of this 
policy.

If a customer of an employer has additional requirements for drug and alcohol testing, policies, or procedures, all 
employees and employers shall abide by such requirements.

<u>Failure to abide by such requirements shall constitute a violation of this policy justifying reassignment or other</u> 
<u>appropriate discipline.</u>

## Disciplinary Action/Policy Violation

For all policy violations, the employee will be referred to the EAP for evaluation. Such employees shall be allowed to 
return to work when the employee has satisfied two requirements:

1. Complete the assessment and develop a treatment plan with the therapist.

2. Pass the Return-to-Work Drug and/or Alcohol Test as interpreted by the MRO.

Ongoing compliance is required in completing the recommended treatment or counseling, if any is required by the EAP 
counselor.

Employees who violate this policy again may be terminated or suspended without pay from employment and may be 
allowed to return to work upon completion of the EAP treatment program.

Employees who violate this policy by selling, manufacturing, or distributing drugs or alcohol at work will be terminated 
from employment.

This policy does not specify a specific suspension from work duties.

## Safe Harbor

If an employee suspects that they have a controlled substance abuse problem, the employee is expected to seek 
assistance. Any employee who voluntarily notifies the employer or the <u>Union</u> that they are seeking assistance, prior to 
being subject to testing under this policy, shall not be in violation of this policy. The employee shall be required to pass 
an <u>RTD</u> Test before returning to work and will be referred to the EAP for counseling.

## Drug Testing Circumstances

<u>Pre-Employment Procedures:</u> All prospective employees joining the pool of employees shall pass a drug test, the 
cost of which is paid for by the DFW Management Fund. Bargaining unit employees shall pass this drug test before 
being dispatched by IBEW, or the UETA to an employer.

---

<u>Post-Accident Tests:</u> Following investigation, an alcohol, and drug screening may be required when a work-related 
incident, near miss situation, violation of the safety precautions, or violation of company policy occurs. In the event of 
an accident which is OSHA/UOSHA recordable and/or which results in damage to property or equipment, any 
person(s) directly involved in the accident may be required to submit to a drug/alcohol test.

<u>Reasonable Suspicion</u> **:** (For-Cause Test) Reasonable Suspicion testing will be done in cases where there is a belief 
by a coworker, company official, safety manager or supervisor that an employee may be using drugs and alcohol while 
at work or reporting to work under the influence of drugs, alcohol, or misusing prescription drugs. Reasonable 
Suspicion or For-Cause tests will be required when there is any of the following:

1. Observable phenomena (actual use or possession) **“**<u>Observed Behavior-Reasonable Suspicion</u><u>”</u> <u>Record</u> 
<u>form*</u>.

2. Two contemporaneous visual behaviors or, in other words simultaneous behavior occurring at the same 
time that leads you to believe that someone might be under the influence.

3. Abnormal behavior or physical characteristics not satisfactorily explained by normal circumstances.

4. A work-related positive test outside of the policy, for example, a customer required drug test may be cause 
for reasonable suspicion.

<u>The DER will oversee the process and sign this Reasonable Suspicion Form</u>.

An employee’s private property may only be inspected for reasonable suspicion/cause and shall include employee’s 
lockers, lunch boxes, toolboxes, backpacks, purses, and the like that are brought by the employee onto the 
employer’s property or used for work related purposes.

<u>Random Testing</u>**:** Random testing will be administered by a third-party administrator retained by the DFW 
Committee and paid for by the DFW Management Fund. Random testing will be conducted for those employees 
covered by the scope of this policy. In the event a random test is out of area (OOA), the employee will be notified and 
be expected to report to the testing site promptly. They will be compensated for their time and travel as per the CBA.

<u>Return to Duty (RTD) and Follow-up:</u> When an employee has violated this policy and has been referred to the <u>EAP</u>
for evaluation, They must pass a RTD drug and/or alcohol test prior to being released by the MRO to return to work, 
the RTD drug test must be a **“**clean**”**negative. If it is the first violation of this policy, passing the return to duty test 
means that a negative result is obtained on the drug/alcohol test for all substances except marijuana. If the 
offending-controlled substance is marijuana, the employee may pass the test if the result shows significantly 
descending levels, indicating the employee is not currently using the controlled substance. Therefore, a positive 
marijuana test can be defined as a passing test by the MRO if the employee verifies no current use, and the 
descending THC levels based on the THC ratio scale and are consistent with the profile of someone who is no longer 
using.

Once the employee returns to work, They will be subject to follow-up tests to verify continued compliance with this 
policy. The <u>EAP</u> will specify the number and frequency of the follow-up tests and the <u>TPA</u> will coordinate the followup testing with the employee. A positive follow-up drug test will be treated as an additional violation of the policy, 
subjecting the employee to the disciplinary actions specified in the policy for subsequent violations.

<u>Contesting a Drug Test Result:</u> An employee will have 72 hours to request the reconfirmation of any specimen that 
is deemed positive, adulterated*, or substituted. The reconfirmation test will be performed at a different SAMHSAcertified laboratory and if practicable on the original specimen having produced enough quantity to perform the 
reconfirmation.

## Procedures

<u>Confidentiality:</u> To ensure the confidentiality of test results and the privacy of employees, all communication 
concerning drug or alcohol testing, sample collection, test results, employee notification, and discipline will be 
handled only by an authorized representative of the employer, Union*,   NECA*,  U E TA*,  TPA*,   EAP*,  o r  t h e  MRO*.  A n y  
electronic transmission of personal information must be treated as confidential; all electronic file transmission will be 
encrypted via password (s) and/or other encryption methods.

<u>Drug testing records will only be accessible to authorized personnel.</u>

<u>Reporting Procedures</u>: Following a random or follow-up drug test an employee shall return to work until notified of 
the test results. If the result is negative, the employee shall continue to work. For any test result that is verified as 
positive, the result will be verbally called by either the MRO, MRO-A or the <u>TPA</u> to the DER and will be followed by an 
electronically transmitted notification of non-compliance on the day that the result is received.

Copies of the notification will also be sent to the EAP, along with the MRO’s remarks for <u>RTD</u> testing protocol. An 
additional copy of the non-compliance notification will be sent to any other designated IBEW or UETA representative. 
Positive alcohol tests will be reported immediately the DER.

Employees who test positive for drugs or alcohol will be removed from the job site by an authorized representative of 
the employer upon receipt of notification of the positive test result. In notifying an employee of a positive test result, 
the authorized representative of the employer shall utilize the written standard form of notification and shall make 
certain that the notification is given to the employee in privacy. This notification will include information concerning 
the EAP and how to contact them. The written notification will be given to the potential employee by an authorized 
representative of the Local Union or the UETA. The employee must have a notification of compliance from the 
TPA/<u>MRO</u> in order to be dispatched by the Local Union.

<u>Pre-Employment Tests:</u> Pre-employment tests are conducted at the third-party administrator’s facility or at a 
designated subcontracted facility under the supervision and in compliance with the third party’s policy and 
procedures. A prospective employee will be given an opportunity to explain to the MRO any circumstance that may 
result in a pre-employment positive test.

A prospective employee who tests positive will have the opportunity to take another pre-employment test after 90 
days unless the prospective employee is a member of IBEW in which case they shall be in violation of this Policy and 
shall be referred to the <u>EAP</u> in accordance with the Disciplinary Procedures*. A prospective employee who is not a 
member will not be referred to the EAP.

<u>Reasonable Suspicion Testing</u> With oversight by the DER, Supervisors will fill out the form “<u>Observed Behavior-</u>
<u>Reasonable Suspicion Record</u>” * before requesting a reasonable suspicion test. The circumstances supporting a 
reasonable suspicion test shall be set forth by completing the narrative portion of the Observed Behavior-Reasonable 
Suspicion Record form. If a second witness is not available to corroborate the request for a test, the test will be based 
on the observations of a single supervisor. A reasonable suspicion test can be conducted at the TPA’s testing facility, 
or on-site. If a reasonable suspicion test is needed, the <u>TPA</u> should be contacted immediately.

When a reasonable suspicion test has been properly ordered, the test will be done as soon as possible and within 32 
hours of notification of the employee by the employer for a drug test and within 8 hours of notification to the employee 
for an alcohol test. No employee will be allowed to proceed to a “Reasonable Suspicion” test on their own. Any employee who is suspected of being under the influence of drugs or alcohol, or who may test positive for alcohol, will 
be offered transportation to their place of residence.

Any employee who is required to submit to a “Reasonable Suspicion” * test will not be allowed to return to work until 
a negative test result is received by the employer.

When an employee is tested under the “Reasonable Suspicion” provision of the Substance Abuse policy and the test 
result is negative, the employee will be compensated for all scheduled lost time by the employer.

<u>Post-Accident Testing:</u> Post-accident drug tests will be conducted within 32 hours of the accident. If a drug test is not 
conducted within 32 hours of the occurrence of the accident all drug testing will stop. If an employee is not allowed to 
return to work until the test result is received and the test result is negative, the employee will be compensated for all 
scheduled lost time. All alcohol testing should take place within 2 hours, but not later than 8 hours after the accident. 
If an alcohol test is not conducted within 8 hours of an accident all alcohol testing will stop. If a post-accident test is 
needed, the third-party administrator should be contacted immediately. Post-accident tests can be conducted either 
at the Employer’s facility or, if the parties are injured and admitted to a hospital, at the hospital if practicable.

<u>esting: Random  T</u> The initial pool of employees subject to random testing will be made up of all employees of 
employers. Names will be randomly selected by computer scientific seed generation once a month for drug and/or 
alcohol testing. The <u>TPA</u> will be responsible for this random selection and testing.

Fifty percent (50%) of the employees in the pool will be tested yearly. The TPA will contact the employers of those 
employees who were selected for random testing and coordinate on the job testing with them.

<u>Return to Duty/Follow up and EAP:</u> Once an employee has been informed of a positive drug test by either MRO, the 
designated representative for the employer, the  UETA, and/or the Local <u>Union</u> it will be the responsibility of the 
employee to contact and set up an appointment with the EAP. At the appointment, the EAP Clinician will do an 
assessment and will specify a plan of treatment for the employee. This treatment plan could include education, EAP 
counseling, various treatment options, and aftercare. The EAP will also set the number of and duration of the Followup tests. This information will be specified on the Assessment and Treatment Plan form, which the employee will be 
asked by the clinician to sign. Once the EAP has signed releases by the employee the Assessment and Treatment Plan 
form will be transmitted to the TPA, designated IBEW/UETA representative(s) and the Designated Employer 
Representative. During the appointment, the EAP will inform the employee of when they can take the Return to Duty 
test, according to the information given to the EAP by the TPA/MRO. The EAP will give the employee the Return to Duty 
Test Request form specifying the date that the employee can retest.

When the MRO receives an oral fluid negative <u>RTD</u> (return to duty) test result from the laboratory it will be reported to 
the employer and the union representative. Upon notification of a negative return to duty test result the employee is 
eligible to return to duty and a compliance letter will be issued by the <u>TPA</u>/MRO. The TPA will coordinate all the followup tests as designated on the Assessment and Treatment form by the EAP with the employee. Failure to comply with 
the Return-to-Work Program, the employee will be non-compliant and the TPA will inform the EAP, the designated 
IBEW/ UETA representative(s), and the Designated Employer Representative of the non-compliance. A noncompliance letter will be issued by the TPA/MRO. The employee must be removed from duty until they are compliant 
with EAP requirements.

## Sampling Procedures

Drug tests, sample collections, chain of custody, alcohol tests and reporting of results will be done in conformity with 
this policy and shall be conducted in accordance with the requirements of Title 34, Chapter 38 of the Utah Code 
Annotated and in accordance with the DHHS Mandatory Guidelines for Federal Drug Testing Programs and all normal 
chain-of-custody procedures used in sample collection.

Any laboratory used for drug testing will be Substance Abuse Mental Health Services  Administration (SAMHSA) 
certified and will comply with all applicable rules and regulations of the Department of Health and Human Services 
(DHHS) for drug testing. All drug tests, alcohol tests and sample collection procedures shall be performed under 
reasonable and sanitary conditions.

<u>Cases of Possible Specimen Tampering</u>: Directly observed collections or oral fluid tests are allowed under the 
following circumstances: (All directly observed specimen collections shall be observed by the same sex)

Ø At the time of specimen collection, if a collector observes any behaviors, materials or finds physical evidence 
which clearly indicates an attempt to tamper with specimen or other clear signs of tampering, it becomes a 
refusal to test, or an oral fluid test will be im-mediately given depending on the collector’s observations.

Ø At the time of collection, if the specimen temperature is outside of acceptable range of 90 to 100 degrees F.

Ø The laboratory reported to the MRO that the specimen was negative-dilute with a creatinine concentration 
greater than or equal to 2 mg/dL, but less than or equal to 5 mg/dL; or

Ø The laboratory reported to the MRO that a specimen is invalid, and the MRO reported that there was not an 
adequate medical explanation for the result.

## Medical Review Officer

In the case of a positive, adulterated, substituted,  or invalid laboratory test result, the employee or prospective 
employee may be advised by the MRO, MRO-A or the <u>TPA</u> by telephone on a confidential basis. The employee shall 
have the right to discuss and explain the results, including the right to advise the MRO of any medication prescribed by 
their physician, which may have affected the results of the test. The MRO shall also review the chain-of-custody 
documentation to ensure compliance with DHHS guidelines and normal chain-of-custody procedures.

<u>The MRO will report a positive test if:</u>

Ø An employee has expressly declined the opportunity to discuss the test result with the MRO.

Ø The employer has successfully contacted the employee and instructed them to contact the MRO and the 
employee has failed to contact the MRO within 3 business days.

Ø Neither the employer nor MRO, after making and documenting all reasonable efforts, has been able to contact 
the employee within 5 days of the date on which the MRO receives the confirmed test result from the 
laboratory.

Ø The positive test result is for a substance that has no valid prescription or valid explanation.
