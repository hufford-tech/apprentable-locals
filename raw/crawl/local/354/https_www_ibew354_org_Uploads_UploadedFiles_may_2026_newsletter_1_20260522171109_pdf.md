## IBEW LOCAL 354

## May 2026 Newsletter

## Business Managers Message

## Hello Brothers and Sisters,

It is very busy here at Local 354. Our work picture has changed drastically over the last couple of months with dozens of unfilled calls and record numbers of monthly dispatches, our total membership is currently at 3537 members. An all-time high for Local 354 membership. It is certainly a great time to be a Union Electrician, and it is a prime opportunity for us to organize unrepresented electrical workers that unfortunately have not yet experienced working under a contract and having the opportunity to have a voice and vote to collectively determine the wages and working conditions in their workplace. These opportunities are extremely rare, and many times Locals miss out because they fail to act on time, I can assure you that Local 354 is capitalizing on the moment and taking full advantage of the opportunity. I am pleased to announce that I have hired Alyssa Dahl as our newest organizer. Alyssa has been very active as a volunteer organizer for years and has always been active throughout the Local. I am excited to have her on staff and know that she will be a great addition to my staff. Also, with the uptake in work in our jurisdiction, it is apparent that we need to hire an additional admin to help handle the increased workload at the Hall. This new person will need to be bilingual in Spanish and English and need to have great translator skills as well as general office skills. We will be posting this job opening very soon. Local 354 will also be hosting a job fair at the Utah Electrical Training Alliance on Thursday, May 28th from 5pm to 8pm. The Local has sent invites to over 9,000 unrepresented workers in the State of Utah. However, word of mouth from our members to these workers continue to be our best organizing tool. I would encourage all members to help with spreading the word to increase participation at this event. Even though we are experiencing tremendous growth, we still need hundreds of new Journeymen to fill our current and future needs. We need everyone helping to accomplish this and change the course on organized labor in the State of Utah. We must be able to provide manpower to our contractors for them to keep bidding work. Failure to organize this type of economy results directly into lost market share. Non signatory contractors are worried as more of their workforce is seeing the advantage of working under a Union contract. I appreciate all that you are doing and keep up the good work.

In Solidarity,
Steve Woodman
