**The Apprenticeship Interview**

Congratulations!  You have qualified to be interviewed for an electrical apprenticeship.  The oral interview is an important step in the application process.  You may have some questions about the interview process, such as:

- 1\) How long will the interview last?
- 2\) Who will interview me?
- 3\) What kinds of questions will be asked?
- 4\) Can I do anything to prepare for the interview?
- 5\) How do I request accommodation for a disability?
- 6\) What happens after the interview?

1\) Most interviews take 10 – 20 minutes.  The interviewers will want you to do most of the talking, but you should try to make your point efficiently.  When you are asked a question, organize your thoughts, and answer the question completely.  Keep to the topic: avoid straying into irrelevant subjects.

2\) You will be interviewed by a committee composed of representatives of the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW) and the National Electrical Contractors Association (NECA). The Committee members are highly experienced electrical professionals. They are aware of the knowledge, skills, abilities, and other characteristics that individuals need to be successful electrical workers. The interview team will consist of six (6) Committee members. Likely, each Committee member will take turns asking you questions during the interview.

3\) The actual interview questions are confidential, but we can tell you a lot about the types of questions that will be asked. The Committee will be asking you questions about how you have handled situations in your life that are relevant to success in an apprenticeship. They will want to know the situation, what you did, and result, when you were faced with these situations at work, in school, or at home. Remember, your response to these questions should have the following three parts: (1) The situation. First, you should briefly describe the situation.  (2) What you did. Second, describe the actions you took - what you did - who you talked to - what you said in that situation.  (3) The result. Finally, tell the committee how it turned out.

4\) Before the interview, think about situations that stand out in your mind because you did something you are proud of; you learned an important lesson; or you had to respond to a real challenge. Don’t spend too much time on a question. Focus on the questions the Committee asks.  Speak clearly and loudly enough for the Committee to hear and understand you. Relax, Relax, Relax. It is an interview not an interrogation.  Get a good night’s rest the day before and put your best foot forward during the interview.

5\) If you are a person with a physical or mental impairment that you believe may affect your ability to complete the interview and wish to request an accommodation to ensure that the interview accurately measures your skills and abilities, please notify the JATC, *well in advance of the interview*.  Notifications given on the day of the interview cannot be addressed that day.

6\) After the interview, within a day or two, you will receive an e-mail from the JATC informing you of your interview score. Please keep in mind, selections are not always made immediately following the interview. Selections are typically made at the end of May or beginning of June each year. You will be placed on the Ranked List according to your interview score and remain on the list for two (2) years.  You will be removed from the list when you are selected, request to be removed, or two years from your interview date.
