# RETIREMENT - IN-SERVICE DISTRIBUTION

On January 1, 2026, the NECA-IBEW Memphis Retirement Plan (The “Plan”) will be amended as follows: 
Under Article III, Eligibility for Benefits, a new section 3.4 titled “In-Service Distribution” shall be added.
Section 3.4 In-Service Distribution

Effective January 1, 2026, an Employee, who has attained the age of 60 and on whose behalf the Trustees maintain an Individual 
Account, may request an “In-Service Distribution” benefit from the Employee’s Individual Account. The “In-Service Distribution” 
benefit may only be requested at the beginning of each calendar year and is limited to $25,000 or less. An Employee is not required to 
cease employment to be eligible for the “In-Service Distribution” benefit.

Under Article 4, Section 4.2 Method of Payment, a new subsection (g) shall be added as follows:

## (g)

## In-Service Distribution

*Notwithstanding any other provision herein, an Employee who has attained the age of 60, but has not yet ceased employment, shall* 
*have the option to request a distribution of $25,000 or less from the Employee’s Accumulated Share at the beginning of each calendar year. In* 
*the event the Employee has a Spouse, the informed consent of the Employee’s Spouse will be required in order for the Employee to elect to* 
*receive an In-Service Distribution of the Employee’s Accumulated Share. Such consent must be in writing and must be witnessed by a Notary* 
*Public or representative of the Plan.*

## OUR HISTORY

The history of the IBEW in Memphis, Tennessee stretches back to January 6, 
1892 when the National Brotherhood of Electrical Workers First Grand 
President Henry Miller, who was the founding father of the brotherhood, 
traveled to Memphis from St. Louis and organized NBEW, Local 6 to fight for 
safer working conditions and better wages for Electrical workers in Memphis. 
In those early years the unionized electrical industry faced stiff opposition, 
particularly in the southern states, and Local Union 6 later went defunct due 
to a lack of membership. Over the years prior to Local 474 there were three 
additional Locals that were chartered and later went defunct, those being 
Local 14, Local 50 and Local 192. When the brotherhood expanded into 
Canada the NBEW changed it's name to the International Brotherhood of 
Electrical Workers in 1899. IBEW Local 474 received it's  charter on January 
13, 1906 and has since grown to be a diverse Local union representing 
workers in varying industries other than construction such as electrical 
manufacturing, government services and electrical cooperatives.

Dear Brothers and Sisters,

I hope this message finds you and your families well. I want to take a moment to talk about 
something that has always been at the heart of our strength as an electrical union: education.

Education is more than learning new skills or keeping up with changes in our trade. It’s about 
empowering ourselves, looking out for one another, and making sure every member has the 
knowledge and confidence to succeed. When we invest in education—whether it’s apprenticeship 
training, continuing education, safety classes, or leadership development—we invest directly in the 
future of our union.

A well-educated membership means safer job sites, higher-quality work, and stronger bargaining 
power. It means we can adapt to new technologies, protect our jurisdiction, and ensure that the 
next generation of electricians is prepared to carry our values forward. Education helps us stand 
united, informed, and ready to face challenges together.

Most importantly, education builds community. Learning alongside one another strengthens our 
connections and reminds us that none of us stands alone. When one member grows, we all grow.

Thank you for your dedication, your hard work, and your commitment to our union. I encourage 
each of you to take advantage of the educational opportunities available and to support one 
another in that journey. Together, through education and solidarity, we continue to build a stronger 
union for today and for the future.

In solidarity,

Noel Sherman
