## DUES - AUTO PAYMENT

Local 474 has a number of members who still have their 
bank send an auto payment computerized check to pay 
their dues. The majority of these members have never 
updated their payment information. Which means 
unless they have a large credit on account, eventually 
their payment will end up with a deficit, resulting in 
their dues not being paid for that month. Any member 
who pays this way should update their payment 
information with their bank. The current amount for 
dues is $64.50 per month.  Anytime there is a dues 
increase, it is the member’s responsibility to ensure their 
bank is submitting the correct amount.

## IBEW PENSION - OSB

When a member retires, they can choose the Optional 
Spouse’s Benefit (OSB) through both the Pension Benefit 
Fund (PBF) and the National Electrical Benefit Fund 
(NEBF). Doing this will pay the benefits at a reduced rate but 
leaves the spouse a continuing monthly benefit if the member 
passes first. If the member’s spouse dies first, or the member 
gets a divorce while receiving PBF benefits, the PBF benefit 
will be adjusted to the original full amount, but only if the 
member notifies the PBF office of the spouse’s death. If the 
member’s spouse dies while the member is receiving NEBF 
benefits, the NEBF benefit will be adjusted to the full amount 
the following month, but only if the member notifies the 
NEBF office of the spouse’s death . If the member gets a 
divorce while receiving NEBF benefits, there are variations 
regarding payments, so the member should notify the NEBF 
office immediately to discuss their options.

# RETIREMENT - IN-SERVICE DISTRIBUTION

On January 1, 2026, the NECA-IBEW Memphis Retirement Plan (The “Plan”) will be amended as follows: 
Under Article III, Eligibility for Benefits, a new section 3.4 titled “In-Service Distribution” shall be added.
Section 3.4 In-Service Distribution

Effective January 1, 2026, an Employee, who has attained the age of 60 and on whose behalf the Trustees maintain an Individual 
Account, may request an “In-Service Distribution” benefit from the Employee’s Individual Account. The “In-Service Distribution” 
benefit may only be requested at the beginning of each calendar year and is limited to $25,000 or less. An Employee is not required to 
cease employment to be eligible for the “In-Service Distribution” benefit.

Under Article 4, Section 4.2 Method of Payment, a new subsection (g) shall be added as follows:

## (g)

## In-Service Distribution

*Notwithstanding any other provision herein, an Employee who has attained the age of 60, but has not yet ceased employment, shall* 
*have the option to request a distribution of $25,000 or less from the Employee’s Accumulated Share at the beginning of each calendar year. In* 
*the event the Employee has a Spouse, the informed consent of the Employee’s Spouse will be required in order for the Employee to elect to* 
*receive an In-Service Distribution of the Employee’s Accumulated Share. Such consent must be in writing and must be witnessed by a Notary* 
*Public or representative of the Plan.*

## OUR HISTORY

The history of the IBEW in Memphis, Tennessee stretches back to January 6, 
1892 when the National Brotherhood of Electrical Workers First Grand 
President Henry Miller, who was the founding father of the brotherhood, 
traveled to Memphis from St. Louis and organized NBEW, Local 6 to fight for 
safer working conditions and better wages for Electrical workers in Memphis. 
In those early years the unionized electrical industry faced stiff opposition, 
particularly in the southern states, and Local Union 6 later went defunct due 
to a lack of membership. Over the years prior to Local 474 there were three 
additional Locals that were chartered and later went defunct, those being 
Local 14, Local 50 and Local 192. When the brotherhood expanded into 
Canada the NBEW changed it's name to the International Brotherhood of 
Electrical Workers in 1899. IBEW Local 474 received it's  charter on January 
13, 1906 and has since grown to be a diverse Local union representing 
workers in varying industries other than construction such as electrical 
manufacturing, government services and electrical cooperatives.

# MESSAGE FROM THE BUSINESS MANAGER

Dear Brothers and Sisters,

I hope this message finds you well. I want to take a moment to recognize the hard work,
dedication, and professionalism each of you brings to our trade every day. As members of this
union, we share a proud tradition of standing together to protect our rights, support one another,
and ensure the highest standards in our industry.

Our strength has always come from unity. and one of the most important ways we demonstrate
that unity is by participating in our union's elections. Voting is not just a right-it is a
responsibility that allows every member to have a voice in the direction of our local and the
future of our trade. The decisions we make together help shape our leadership, our priorities, and
the strength of our union for years to come.

As we approach the upcoming election, I strongly encourage every member to make sure your
contact information and mailing address are current with the union hall. Having an accurate
address on file ensures that you receive all important information, notices, and voting materials
related to the election. Staying informed and making sure your voice is heard is essential to
keeping our union strong and representative of all its members.

I encourage every member to stay engaged, look out for your fellow workers, and continue
upholding the values of safety, craftsmanship, and solidarity. Whether you are a seasoned
journeyman or just starting your career, your involvement matters and your vote helps shape the
future of our local.

Thank you for the work you do every day and for the pride you bring to this trade. When we
stand together, stay informed, and participate, we ensure a strong and united future for all our
members.

In solidarity,

Noel Sherman
