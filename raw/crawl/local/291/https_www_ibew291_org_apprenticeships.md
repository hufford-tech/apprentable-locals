# Apprenticeships

## Inside Wireman \| Electrician Apprenticeship

SW Idaho Electrical JATC \| Electrical Construction Training

An electrical apprenticeship program is a structured training opportunity that combines on-the-job experience with classroom instruction to help individuals learn the skills and knowledge necessary to become licensed electricians. Participants in this program work under the supervision of experienced professionals, gaining hands-on experience while learning to install, repair, and maintain electrical systems in residential, commercial, and industrial settings.

The program spans 4 years. During this time, apprentices receive a combination of practical experience and technical education. The technical instruction covers topics like electrical theory, wiring, safety standards, electrical codes, and troubleshooting techniques.

In addition to practical and classroom learning, apprentices take part in safety training, learning how to work safely around high-voltage systems and other electrical hazards. Upon completion of the program, apprentices typically must pass an exam to become a certified or licensed electrician.

The apprenticeship provides an excellent opportunity for individuals to earn a wage while they learn, with compensation often increasing as apprentices advance through the program. Additionally, graduates of the program can pursue a wide range of career opportunities in the electrical trade, with the potential to specialize in fields such as industrial electrical systems, renewable energy, or electrical contracting.

## Substation Tech Apprenticeship

Electrical substations and switchyards are an essential part of the electrical grid that supplies power to consumers wherever and whenever it is needed. Well-trained, high-quality Substation Journeyman are needed to build, maintain, and monitor the equipment inside a substation to ensure utilities can provide reliable power to its customers.​

The Substation Apprenticeship Program is designed to train and supply competent members of the IBEW (International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers) to our NECA Contractors (National Electrical Contractors Association).

Substation Apprentices will earn while they learn, meaning they will get paid while receiving on-the-job training. To gain the on-the-job work experience needed Substation Apprentices will be required to travel to various locations throughout the country, and possibly be employed by multiple contractors. On the job training will focus on topics such as foundations, grounding, fencing, layout and planning, steel erection, wiring, bus welding, conduit installation, transformers, and other functions needed to become a competent Substation Journeyman.  This work will be performed outside in all types of weather. Substation workers are required to work in trenches, at heights, and be physically able to perform the work.​

As a Substation apprentice, attendance will be required at all related training, certification, and qualification classes. All course material must be completed as scheduled. The training received will enhance the opportunity for success in the electrical substation construction industry.

## High Voltage Line Construction Apprenticeship

Mountain States Line Constructors

A high voltage line construction apprenticeship is a specialized training program designed to prepare individuals for careers in building, maintaining, and repairing high-voltage electrical transmission and distribution lines. This apprenticeship combines hands-on field experience with classroom instruction to ensure apprentices gain both the practical skills and technical knowledge needed to work safely and effectively in this demanding field.

Typically lasting 4 years, the program involves on-the-job training under the guidance of experienced journeymen and industry professionals. Apprentices work in various weather conditions and locations, learning to install and maintain power lines, utility poles, and substations. Key tasks include working with high-voltage equipment, installing electrical conductors, troubleshooting issues with transmission systems, and ensuring compliance with safety protocols and industry standards.

Classroom instruction is an integral part of the apprenticeship, covering essential topics such as electrical theory, safety practices, line construction techniques, electrical codes, and the operation of specialized equipment like bucket trucks and cranes. Safety training is a top priority, as apprentices must understand how to work safely with high-voltage systems and be prepared to handle emergency situations.

Upon successful completion of the apprenticeship, individuals typically become journeyman linemen, qualified to work on high-voltage lines and electrical systems. This apprenticeship offers apprentices the opportunity to earn a wage while they learn, with pay often increasing as they progress through the program. Graduates can pursue a variety of career paths, including positions with utility companies, electrical contractors, and in the renewable energy sector, which often requires high-voltage expertise for wind and solar power systems

## Voice \| Data \| Video Construction Apprenticeship

SW Idaho Electrical JATC \| Electrical Construction Training

A Low Voltage Data Systems Apprenticeship Program is a comprehensive training opportunity designed to prepare individuals for careers in the installation, maintenance, and troubleshooting of low-voltage electrical systems used for data transmission, telecommunications, and security systems. This apprenticeship combines on-the-job learning with formal classroom instruction to equip apprentices with the skills and knowledge needed to work on systems like fiber optics, network cables, security alarms, audio-visual equipment, and fire alarms.

Typically lasting 3 years, the apprenticeship program offers a balance of practical experience and technical training. Apprentices work under the supervision of experienced technicians, gaining hands-on experience in tasks such as running and terminating cables, installing wiring for data and communication networks, configuring and maintaining networking equipment, and ensuring systems meet safety and quality standards.

The classroom component of the program covers key topics such as low-voltage electrical theory, network architecture, wiring standards, telecommunications regulations, and the operation of specialized tools and equipment. Safety training is also a critical part of the curriculum, as apprentices learn how to work in a variety of environments, from commercial buildings to industrial settings, while ensuring that they follow the necessary electrical codes and safety protocols.

Upon completion of the program, apprentices typically earn certification or licensure as low-voltage technicians, qualifying them to work independently or as part of a team to install, repair, and maintain low-voltage systems. Graduates of the apprenticeship can pursue careers with telecommunications companies, security firms, IT and network service providers, and in sectors like healthcare, education, and commercial real estate, where low-voltage systems are crucial for operations. Additionally, the program offers opportunities for specialization in areas such as smart building technology, network systems integration, or home automation.
