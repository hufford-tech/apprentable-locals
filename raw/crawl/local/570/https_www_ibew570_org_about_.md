### IBEW 570 History.

![Local Lineman Henry Miller](https://www.ibew570.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Henry_Miller-300x300.jpg)While working at the 1890 St. Louis Exposition, Henry Miller, a local lineman, instigated spirited conversations with fellow tradesmen. They soon found common ground regarding industry-wide problems, such as exceptionally high mortality rates of nearly one for every two hires and earnings of no more than $.15-.20/hour for 12-hour workdays. Wiremen fared no better. The group turned to the American Federation of Labor and chartered themselves as the Electrical Wiremen and Linemen’s Union, No. 5221 of the AFL.

On November 21, 1891, the National Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (NBEW) was formed at a convention with 10 delegates. Henry Miller was elected president of the newly formed NBEW. The delegates drafted a Constitution and laws while also creating a symbolic emblem of a fist holding lightning bolts.

The core objectives of the NBEW established nearly 130 years ago still stand today. They are as follows:

To promote reasonable methods of work

To cultivate feelings of friendship among those in our industry

To settle all disputes between employers and employees by arbitration ( _if possible_)

To assist each other in sickness and distress

To secure employment

To reduce the hours of daily labor

To secure adequate pay for our work

To seek a higher and higher standard of living

To seek security for the individual

[![IBEW Local Union 570 History](https://www.ibew570.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/History_and_of_IBEW-1-300x300.jpg)](https://youtu.be/mUgfpvrGB50) In 1899, at the convention in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, the NBEW’s name was officially changed to the **International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, IBEW.**

Today, IBEW represents approximately 750,000 members who work in a wide variety of fields, including construction, utilities, telecommunications, broadcasting, manufacturing, railroads, and government.
