01 — Logo
The mark
The logo and the full asset package are locked. A red ladder sits at the seam of the name, splitting apprent from able — the mark is the word's own hinge, not an ornament placed beside it.
Primary lockup · light backgrounds
Reversed lockup · navy and dark backgrounds
One-color · Blueprint
Single-color print, embroidery, laser etch, fax-grade reproduction.
One-color · White
Dark garments, photography, any surface where color can’t be trusted.
01
The ladder
Signal Red, four rungs, leaning forward at roughly 12°. Trade-agnostic by design — it reads as apprenticeship in any craft, not electrical alone.
02
The split
Blueprint navy carries apprent; Live Wire gold carries able. Navy is where you start, gold is what you become. Never invert the two.
03
Lowercase always
The wordmark is set lowercase with no period and no space around the ladder. Never set the name in title case or all caps in logo position.
Why the ladder scales
A ladder belongs to every trade and to none. When Apprentable extends beyond electrical to plumbing, HVAC, or pipefitting, the mark does not change — only the product label beside it does. There is no second logo, ever. Sub-trades are named in UI text, not in the identity.

01.2 — Rules
Clear space and minimum size
Clear space on all four sides equals the height of the ladder's top rung. Nothing enters this zone — no text, no rules, no photo edges, no other logos.
ApplicationMinimum size
Full lockup · digital140 px wide. Below this the rungs close up and the ladder reads as a solid bar.
Full lockup · print1.25 in wide.
Ladder icon · digital16 px. Use the purpose-built favicon file, not a downscaled lockup.
Do
  • Use the full-color lockup on white or Drafting Paper wherever possible.
  • Use the reversed lockup on navy and dark surfaces. Over photography, place it on a solid dark area, never over detail.
  • Use the ladder alone whenever the space is square or under 140px wide.
  • Keep the ladder's lean and rung count exactly as drawn.
Do not
  • Swap navy and gold between apprent and able.
  • Recolor the ladder to gold, navy, or anything other than Signal Red or solid white.
  • Add space, a hyphen, or a slash around the ladder — it must stay letter-tight.
  • Straighten the ladder, add rungs, or stretch the lockup non-proportionally.
  • Set the mark inside a circular seal, ring, or badge. That silhouette belongs to trade unions.
  • Add a shadow, outline, gradient, or bevel in any application.

02 — Brand Foundation
Who we are
Apprentable exists to close the preparation gap for workers trying to enter the skilled trades. Our identity is built at the intersection of heritage craft authority and modern utility — serious, optimistic, and earned.
01
Earned, not given
We don't promise shortcuts. We give people the tools to walk in prepared and walk out with the job.
02
Craft meets technology
Heritage vocabulary — journeymen, apprentices, locals — treated with respect, not irony. AI in service of real-world skill.
03
Serious optimism
Navy is where you start, gold is what you become. We don’t hype — we equip. The brand should feel like a tool, not a pitch.
04
Spare and precise
White space is a craft value. No clutter, no noise. Every element earns its place — like a clean electrical diagram.
03 — Color Palette
Colors
A three-color system drawn from the visual language of American trade work — navy for structure, gold for progress, red for signal. Three primary colors carry semantic roles; five supporting colors complete the system. Apprentable is independent and unaffiliated with any union or JATC, and the palette is never presented as derived from one.

06 — Voice and tone
How we write
Our voice reflects the trades world: direct, no-nonsense, and quietly confident. We never hype, never condescend, and never use the word "empower."
Direct over clever
"Practice the real test" beats "unlock your potential." Say what the thing does. Name it by what the user controls, not how it's built.
Claim the process, not the outcome
Claim what we do, never what they’ll get. "Your diagnostic shows exactly which categories you’re weakest in" — not "most applicants improve." We publish no outcome statistic until we have one, and when we do, the sample size ships with it.
Heritage with respect
Use trades vocabulary — journeyman, JATC, local, apprentice — without irony or explanation. Our audience knows these words. Use them fluently.
Sentence case everywhere
"Start practicing" not "Start Practicing." Buttons, headings, nav labels — always sentence case. All-caps only for eyebrow labels and metadata.
05
Nominative use only
Always "IBEW aptitude test prep," never "IBEW-approved," "official," or "partnered." We describe what we prepare people for. We never imply endorsement by a union, a local, or a JATC.
06
American spelling, sentence case
Color, not colour. Sentence case for buttons, headings, and nav. All-caps reserved for eyebrows and metadata. Spell out "and" in headings rather than using an ampersand.
