# Unexpected electrician apprenticeship insights

## Scope

This analysis covers all 778,173 records coded to O*NET-SOC **47-2111.00 Electricians** in the Apprentices file. Electrical power-line installers and repairers (49-9051.00) are excluded. Official counts use federal fiscal year 2025; demographic and wage trends compare calendar-year starts.

## What stands out

### 1. Electrician apprenticeship starts more than doubled

Calendar-year electrician starts grew from **22,303 in 2015 to 50,012 in 2025**, an increase of **124.2%**. Starts across all occupations grew **73.8%** over the same period. Electricians consequently rose from **12.35% to 15.93% of all apprenticeship starts**.

### 2. The new electrician pipeline is younger

The median age at registration fell from **26 in 2015 to 24 in 2025**. Across all occupations, the corresponding median moved from 27 to 26. Electrician apprenticeship is therefore attracting entrants two years younger than the system-wide median.

### 3. Women remain unusually underrepresented—and the gap widened

Among starts with an identified sex, women's share of electrician starts rose from **3.95% in 2015 to 5.77% in 2025**. The overall apprenticeship system rose from **10.60% to 18.43%**. Electrician growth has therefore not translated into comparable gender diversification.

### 4. Hispanic representation nearly reached the system-wide level

Among starts with identified ethnicity, the Hispanic share of electrician starts increased from **21.29% to 30.46%** between 2015 and 2025. The 2025 all-occupation figure was **31.55%**, leaving only a 1.10 percentage-point gap.

### 5. Recorded electrician starting wages were below the overall median

For hourly records, the 2025 median starting wage was **$19.00 for electricians versus $20.60 across all occupations**. It was also lower in 2015: $13.00 versus $15.31. These are nominal wages recorded at apprenticeship registration—not journey-level wages, inflation-adjusted earnings, benefits, or lifetime compensation.

### 6. "Electrician apprenticeship" is a much broader market than union JATCs

Official FY2025 data show **48,418 new electrician apprentices**, or **15.39% of all new apprentices**. The program mix was:

- **18,354 (37.9%)** in multi-employer programs with a union, CBA, or joint-training signal
- **17,163 (35.4%)** in other multi-employer programs
- **10,220 (21.1%)** in single-employer programs
- **2,346 (4.8%)** in USMAP military programs
- **335 (0.7%)** other or unknown

Separately, **19,449 records (40.2%)** carried an explicit union flag. The file therefore captures union JATCs, IEC/ABC-style programs, single employers, and military training—not simply one pathway or organization.

### 7. Texas is the geographic outlier

Texas registered **5,926 new electrician apprentices in FY2025**, 12.2% of the national total and more than twice Florida's 2,947. Ohio followed with 2,667, California with 2,487, and Virginia with 2,291. The dataset also contains a 2,486-record national/military bucket coded `ZA`, which should not be treated as a state.

## A strong lead that needs deeper validation

For 2015–2019 electrician starts whose current status had resolved to completed or cancelled, the completion share was **65.4%** in the heuristic union/CBA/joint-training group, compared with **33.0%** in other multi-employer programs, **43.3%** in single-employer programs, and **20.8%** in USMAP.

This is an exploratory comparison, **not an official completion rate or causal finding**. Program mix, reporting practices, transfers, follow-up time, and military administration may differ. It is best treated as a promising question for a separately controlled analysis.

## Method notes

- Source snapshot date: August 17, 2026.
- Official FY2025 new, active, and exiter counts use the source's fiscal-year flags.
- Trends use calendar-year apprenticeship start dates, so the 2025 trend count and FY2025 official count are not expected to match.
- Demographic shares exclude unknown or unreported values from their denominators.
- Program categories are descriptive heuristics and are not audited classifications of IBEW affiliation.
